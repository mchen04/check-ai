# Anti-AI Design: Making Landing Pages Feel Human, Not Template-Generated

## The AI Template Aesthetic (What to Avoid)

AI-generated and template landing pages share recognizable patterns that scream "low effort":

### Visual Red Flags
- **Gradient blobs** - Purple/pink/blue gradients floating in backgrounds
- **Generic 3D illustrations** - Those floating humans, abstract shapes, isometric icons
- **Excessive glassmorphism** - Everything has blur, transparency, and frost effects
- **Rainbow gradients** - Especially in text or buttons
- **Dark mode with neon accents** - The "AI startup" look
- **Abstract particle effects** - Floating dots, connecting lines
- **Oversized hero sections** - Massive padding with little content

### Copy Red Flags
- **"Supercharge your..."** - The most overused phrase in SaaS
- **"The future of..."** - Vague, says nothing
- **"Revolutionary/Game-changing"** - Empty superlatives
- **"Unlock the power of..."** - Template speak
- **"Seamlessly integrate..."** - Everyone claims this
- **"AI-powered everything"** - AI fatigue is real
- **"Boost productivity 10x"** - Unsubstantiated claims

### Structural Red Flags
- **Feature grid with icons** - Same 3-4 column grid everywhere
- **Testimonial carousels** - Especially with stock photo avatars
- **Generic "How it works" 1-2-3** - Without real specificity
- **Team section with placeholder bios** - "Jane is passionate about..."
- **FAQ accordion with obvious questions** - Padding content

## Authentic Design Principles

### 1. Specific Over Generic

**Generic**: "Powerful AI verification for modern teams"
**Specific**: "7 research-backed methods to catch AI hallucinations before deployment"

**Generic**: Gradient blob background
**Specific**: Clean background with your actual product screenshot

**Generic**: "Get started" button
**Specific**: "Verify your first AI output free"

The rule: if it could apply to any product, it's too generic.

### 2. Restraint Over Decoration

Every design element should have a job. Ask: "Does this help the user understand or convert?"

**Remove**:
- Decorative illustrations that don't explain anything
- Gradient backgrounds that add visual noise
- Animations that distract from content
- Multiple competing visual focal points

**Keep**:
- Product screenshots/demos
- Icons that clarify (not decorate)
- Subtle hover states
- One animated element that demonstrates value

### 3. Real Imagery Over Stock

Stock photos are instantly recognizable and destroy trust. Instead:
- **Product screenshots** - Actual UI, not mockups
- **Code snippets** - Real API examples
- **Diagrams you made** - Custom flowcharts, architecture
- **Founder photos** - Real faces, casual settings

If you must use illustrations, make them custom or at least distinctive. Avoid Lottie-style, Blush-style, or generic isometric libraries.

### 4. Typography That Signals Craft

Template typography is detectable:
- **Default system fonts** - Usually indicate low effort
- **Overused Google Fonts** - Poppins, Inter, Montserrat (not bad, just common)
- **Inconsistent sizing** - Random heading sizes
- **Poor line length** - Lines too long or too short

Better approaches:
- **Pick one font, use it well** - Inter is fine if applied thoughtfully
- **Tight line lengths** - 45-75 characters for body text
- **Intentional weight progression** - Clear hierarchy between H1, H2, H3
- **Letter-spacing refinement** - Tighter tracking on headlines

### 5. Color That Doesn't Scream "Template"

AI/template colors to avoid:
- Purple/violet gradients (Framer template default)
- Bright teal/cyan neon (Web3 aesthetic)
- Rainbow gradients (pride flag as design choice)
- Pure black backgrounds (#000000)
- Gray-only palettes (emotionless)

Better color approaches:
- **One distinctive brand color** - Use sparingly, not everywhere
- **Warm neutrals** - Slightly warm grays feel more human than pure gray
- **Intentional accent** - One pop color for CTAs, used consistently
- **Avoid gradient overuse** - If you use gradients, be subtle

### 6. Whitespace as a Design Statement

Templates often cram content to seem "feature-rich." Human design breathes:
- **Generous section padding** - Let each section stand alone
- **Visual hierarchy through space** - Important things get more space around them
- **Consistent rhythm** - Predictable spacing creates calm
- **Empty space is a choice** - Not filling space signals confidence

### 7. Micro-Details That Signal Craft

Small touches that separate hand-crafted from template:
- **Custom favicon** - Not the default framework icon
- **Smooth scrolling** - Polished scroll behavior
- **Hover states** - Thoughtful, not just color change
- **Loading states** - If something loads, show care
- **Error states** - Helpful, not default browser errors
- **Cursor changes** - Pointer on clickable, text on selectable
- **Focus states** - Accessible and visible

### 8. Conversational Copy

Template copy is stiff and formal. Human copy is direct:

**Template**: "Our platform leverages cutting-edge AI technology to deliver superior verification outcomes."
**Human**: "We check if your AI is making things up."

**Template**: "Begin your journey toward AI excellence today."
**Human**: "Try it free. No credit card needed."

**Template**: "Trusted by industry-leading organizations worldwide."
**Human**: "47 teams shipped with us last month."

Rules for human copy:
- Write like you talk
- Use "you" and "your" more than "our" and "we"
- Specific numbers beat vague claims
- Short sentences beat long ones
- Active voice beats passive

## Specific Anti-Patterns to Kill

### Headlines to Never Use
- "Supercharge your [anything]"
- "The future of [anything]"
- "Next-generation [anything]"
- "[Something] reimagined"
- "Unlock the power of..."
- "[Something], but better"
- "AI-powered [basic thing]"

### Design Elements to Avoid
- Animated gradient backgrounds
- Floating 3D objects
- Particle effects
- Auto-playing videos with no context
- Testimonial carousels (especially with stock photos)
- Generic feature icons (checkmarks, lightning bolts, shields)
- "Trusted by" sections with fake-looking logos

### Structural Cliches to Rethink
- Hero → Features Grid → Testimonials → CTA (too predictable)
- Three pricing columns with "Popular" badge
- "How it works" with numbered circles
- FAQ accordion with padding questions

## Building Authentic Differentiation

### Show Your Actual Product
Nothing builds trust like showing what you built. Screenshots, code examples, real output.

### Let Your Expertise Show
Technical depth signals competence. Don't dumb down for SEO.

### Embrace Personality
A point of view is differentiation. Take a stance on your industry.

### Details Over Polish
A slightly rough site with real content beats a polished template with generic content.

### Speed Over Animation
Fast, simple sites convert better than slow, animated ones.

## The Human Test

Before shipping, ask:
1. Could this page belong to any competitor? (If yes, it's too generic)
2. Does every image serve a purpose? (If not, remove it)
3. Would I believe this copy if I didn't work here? (If not, rewrite it)
4. Is there any element that's there "because landing pages have this"? (If yes, remove it)
5. Does it load fast on a phone? (If not, simplify it)
