What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.
Check is an API that verifies AI-generated content for accuracy. Like Stripe offers credit cards, ACH, and crypto for payments, we offer 7 verification methods: semantic similarity, entropy checking, LLM-as-judge reasoning, bidirectional premise reasoning, tool-augmented web search, multi-model ensemble voting, and formal mathematical proofs.

Customers pick which methods to use based on their needs. A legal AI company might use 50% ensemble + 50% tool-augmented to catch both reasoning errors and fake citations. A medical AI might use 60% BiPRM + 30% tool + 10% formal. A blog writer uses semantic only for speed.

One API call. Choose your methods. Get a verdict (TRUE/FALSE/UNCERTAIN) with confidence score and explanation.

Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making?
Justin (Mastercard compliance, 400+ application audits): Saw enterprise paranoia about AI accuracy firsthand. Companies won't deploy AI at scale without verification—it's a compliance blocker.

Michael (autonomous AI agents at Epoch, DOD sensor systems): Saw cascading failure problem. One hallucination in an agent workflow breaks code, breaks subsequent decisions. Built AI systems where accuracy was non-negotiable.

Market validation: Air Canada chatbot invented a refund policy (Feb 2024), customer sued and won—established legal precedent. FDA (Jan 2025) mandated verification for medical AI. CA AB 3030 (Jan 2026) requires disclosure of AI-generated healthcare content. 78% of enterprises now use AI in production.

Competitors (Guardrails AI $7.5M, Cleanlab $25M, Harmonic $1.45B valuation) prove the market exists. But they all force one verification approach. We're the only multi-method platform.

Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?
Guardrails AI ($7.5M seed): Rule-based safety only. Can't verify factual accuracy.
Cleanlab ($25M Series A): Single trustworthiness score. Can't customize per use case.
Harmonic ($295M, $1.45B valuation): Math proofs only. 99% of use cases aren't math.
AIMon: Single detection model.

What they all miss: Single-method verification is fundamentally brittle. When that one method fails (misses a negation, hallucinates a citation), nothing catches it. Different domains need different verification approaches. A medical AI needs different checks than a legal AI than a coding assistant.

We're the only platform offering multiple methods with custom weighting. Customers can combine methods to match their specific accuracy requirements.
