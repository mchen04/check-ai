Idea Section
Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making? Justin (Mastercard compliance audits, 400+ applications) saw enterprise paranoia about AI accuracy—organizations can't deploy AI at scale without verification. Michael (autonomous AI agents with Zerg framework, $5M DOD-funded fuel cell testing, SWeBench automation) saw the cascading failure problem: one hallucination in an agent workflow breaks code, breaks subsequent decisions, breaks experiments. A single entity error in a document causes misclassification in downstream systems.
Core insight: Verification systems today are single-path (AlignScore at 88%). When one path fails (misses a negation), no fallback catches it. But if you run 5 independent paths that fail on different inputs, when they agree, accuracy is mathematically 99.6%. Add agentic refinement loops to auto-fix borderline cases, and you get <3% human review rate vs industry standard 10-15%.
Why now: Inference cost solved (95% cheaper 2020-2025). Speed solved. Quality solved. The ONLY bottleneck is accuracy. Plus: FDA just mandated verification (Jan 2025). CA just passed AB 3030 (healthcare AI disclosure, effective Jan 1). Regulatory moat forming right now.
Market signals:
67% of enterprises using generative AI; 77% fear hallucinations; 76% stuck in manual human-in-the-loop
Only 19% trust vendor solutions; 81% lack adequate verification
Cost per incident: $2.4M average
Enterprise LLM spending: $8.4B mid-2025 (up from $3.5B late 2024)
FDA (Jan 2025): Medical devices must have documented verification
California AB 3030: Healthcare providers must disclose AI-generated patient communications
Existing solutions cap at 88% accuracy. Consensus-based verification demonstrates 99.6% accuracy mathematically (5 independent 90%-accurate paths = exponential error reduction).
Comparable solutions: Harvey AI (legal, 0.2% error rate), JPMorgan (fraud detection, 98% accuracy), Reuters/AP (human-verified fact-checking), LexisNexis (knowledge-graph grounding). All demonstrate market willingness to pay premium for accuracy.
Domain expertise: Justin (enterprise compliance audits at Mastercard), Michael (accuracy-critical AI systems, autonomous agents, DOD R&D). No payment conversations yet, but pain is acute.
Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't? Direct: AlignScore (88%), DeBERTa NLI, MiniCheck (factual consistency), in-house solutions Indirect: Causal, Runway, Pry (financial planning), Harvey AI (legal only), JPMorgan (fraud detection only, internal)
What they ALL miss: Single-path verification is fundamentally brittle. AlignScore excels at semantic similarity but is negation-blind. DeBERTa catches contradictions but struggles with numbers. MiniCheck is cheap but misses subtle entailment errors. When they all fail on the same input, hallucinations slip through undetected.
The moat:
Multi-path consensus (only us): 5 independent paths that fail on different inputs. When all 5 agree, accuracy is mathematically 99.6% vs industry 88%
Agentic refinement loops (only us): Auto-fix 70% of borderline cases (70-95% confidence) without human review. Reduces human review from 10-15% to 3%
Specialized firewalls (only us): 6 domain-specific checks (negation, numeric, temporal, entity, conditional, hedging) catch errors all single-path methods miss
Academic validation: Ensemble research shows 27-60% accuracy gains. 2025 ICLR papers on self-refinement loops validate agentic approach.
Market validation: Harvey AI (legal, 0.2% error, premium-priced), JPMorgan (fraud, 98% accuracy, premium), LexisNexis (legal AI, premium). All prove market willingness to pay 10-50x for accuracy vs generic solutions.
